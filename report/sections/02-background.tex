\section{Literature Review and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Related work.} Curtmola et al.\ defined the security of SSE and built schemes with an encrypted
index~\cite{curtmola2006sse}. Cash et al.\ built dynamic schemes for very large databases from counter-based
dictionaries~\cite{cash2014dynamic}, and Stefanov et al.\ and Bost made forward privacy a design
goal~\cite{stefanov2014practical,bost2016sophos}. On the password side, Jarecki et al.\ built password-protected
secret sharing from a threshold oblivious PRF (TOPRF)~\cite{jarecki2016highly}, and Chen et al.\ applied it to
searchable encryption in PASE~\cite{chen2021pase}. Roy et al.\ and Jiang et al.\ moved password-protected secret
sharing to lattices~\cite{roy2021pps,jiang2024qpause}, and QPASE adds keyword search~\cite{jiang2024qpase}.

\paragraph{Ring LWE and the lattice PRF.} All lattice operations take place in $\Rq$, where a product wraps around
with $X^n = -1$. Security rests on ring learning with errors~\cite{regev2009lwe,lyubashevsky2010ring}: pairs
$(a, as + e)$ with small $s, e$ look uniform. QPASE uses the Banerjee--Peikert PRF~\cite{banerjee2014prf}. For an
input $x \in \{0,1\}^L$,
\begin{equation}
a_x = a_{x_1} \, G^{-1}\!\big(a_{x_2} \, G^{-1}( \cdots G^{-1}(a_{x_L}))\big),
\qquad F_k(x) = \big\lfloor (p/q) \cdot a_x \cdot k \big\rceil .
\label{eq:bp-prf}
\end{equation}
Computing $a_x$ takes $L - 1$ products of a vector with an $\ell \times \ell$ matrix of ring elements. We call one
evaluation of $a_x$ a \emph{PRF chain}. It dominates the cost of the whole scheme.

\paragraph{Threshold OPRF.} Server $S_i$ holds a share $k_i$ of the master key $K$ and publishes $pk_i = ak_i +
2e_i$. The user blinds its input as $b = ar + a_x + 2e$, each of $t$ servers returns $b_{k_i} = bk_i + 2e'_i$, and
with the Lagrange coefficients the user computes
\begin{equation}
F = \sum_i \lambda_i b_{k_i} - r \sum_i \lambda_i pk_i = a_x K + 2e'' .
\label{eq:toprf-combine}
\end{equation}
The noise $2e''$ changes at every login. The robust extractor of Ding et al.~\cite{ding2012kex} removes it: from a
hint $\sigma = S(F)$ stored at Register, $\Ku = E(F, \sigma)$ is the same in every login. The servers create $K$ by
a distributed key generation with Shamir sharing, so $K$ is never assembled anywhere.

\paragraph{Symmetric and signature primitives.} Keys come from HKDF~\cite{krawczyk2010hkdf}, signatures use
ML-DSA-44~\cite{fips204}, and files and index entries use AES-256-GCM~\cite{dworkin2007gcm}. In QPASE, HKDF and the
PRF are both built on the lattice PRF.
